1. Venture Capital Investors

Venture capital (VC) is investment capital provided to high-growth startups that have the potential to become large businesses.

  • VC investors generally invest through investment funds.
  • They look for businesses with high growth potential.
  • VC funding can occur through Seed, Series A, Series B and later rounds.
  • In return, investors normally receive an ownership stake in the company.

2. Angel Investors

Angel investors are generally individuals who invest their own money in startups, particularly during early stages of development.

  • Usually individual investors.
  • Often invest earlier than large VC funds.
  • May provide money, experience and business contacts.
  • They normally receive equity in return for investment.

4. How to Read PitchBook

PitchBook Founder Matrix should not be interpreted as a conventional academic ranking. It provides a different perspective: the entrepreneurial and venture-capital footprint associated with university alumni.

  • Undergraduates: founders associated with undergraduate education.
  • Graduates: founders associated with graduate-level education other than the MBA category.
  • MBA: founders associated with MBA programmes.
  • Founder Count: number of founders represented in the relevant PitchBook dataset.
  • Company Count: number of companies associated with those founders.
  • Capital Raised: venture capital raised by the companies represented in the dataset.

What Does Capital Raised Mean?

Capital raised represents investment obtained by the relevant companies. It is not the university's revenue, the university's endowment or the founders' personal wealth.

What Does Founder Count Mean?

Founder Count should be read as a measure of founders represented in the relevant PitchBook dataset. It does not mean that every entrepreneur produced by a university is necessarily included.
